Section 1

Introduction to the Circulatory System

The circulatory system, also known as the cardiovascular system, is a vast network of organs and blood vessels that acts as the primary transport system of the human body. It is responsible for delivering vital oxygen, hormones, and nutrients to cells, and collecting carbon dioxide and metabolic waste products for elimination. Without the continuous operation of this system, body cells would quickly deteriorate due to starvation and toxic waste build-up.

🫀
Section 2

Heart Anatomy & Physiology

The heart is a muscular organ containing four chambers: two upper atria (receiving chambers) and two lower ventricles (pumping chambers). Blood flow direction is strictly controlled by four specialized one-way valves (tricuspid, pulmonary, mitral, aortic), preventing backflow during contraction. The right side pumps oxygen-poor blood to the lungs (pulmonary circuit), while the left side distributes oxygen-rich blood to the rest of the body (systemic circuit).

🛣️
Section 3

The Vascular System: Blood Vessels

Blood flows through three primary vascular networks: Arteries carry oxygenated blood away from the heart at high pressures, featuring thick muscular walls; Veins return deoxygenated blood back to the heart at low pressures using skeletal muscle pumps and one-way valves to combat gravity; and microscopic Capillaries act as exchange junctions, enabling nutrients and gases to diffuse directly between blood and surrounding tissues.

🩸
Section 4

Blood Components & Composition

Blood is a specialized liquid tissue comprised of four main elements: Red Blood Cells (RBCs) containing hemoglobin for oxygen transport; White Blood Cells (WBCs) acting as the immune system's defensive shields; Platelets which trigger coagulation to seal vascular ruptures; and Plasma, a straw-colored liquid consisting of 90% water alongside dissolved hormones, clotting factors, nutrients, and carbon dioxide.

💡
Section 5

Interesting Facts about Circulation

Did you know that the human heart beats about 100,000 times a day, pumping around 2,000 gallons of blood through your body? Furthermore, if all the blood vessels in an adult human body were laid out end-to-end, they would stretch for over 60,000 miles—enough to circle the Earth more than twice! Remarkably, the heart can continue beating even when separated from the body, as long as it has an adequate oxygen supply, due to its intrinsic electrical pacemaker.
